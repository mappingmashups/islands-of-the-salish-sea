The 100 Largest Islands
of the Salish Sea

Each island drawn at the same map scale, arranged largest to smallest.

About the poster

The Salish Sea is the inland sea shared by Washington State and British Columbia, encompassing Puget Sound, the Strait of Georgia, and the Strait of Juan de Fuca. It contains hundreds of islands; this poster shows the 100 largest by area, each rendered at the same map scale so their relative sizes are immediately apparent.

Islands are drawn in their north-up orientation and arranged in reading order, largest to smallest, left to right. US islands are shown in a warmer green; Canadian islands in a cooler grey-green.

Data from the WWU Salish Sea Institute and the Mapterhorn project.

How it's made

The poster is generated entirely in Python using GeoPandas and matplotlib. Islands are packed using a shelf bin-packing algorithm with left-kerning compaction: each island is nudged leftward until its true polygon edge sits within a target gap of its neighbour, letting complex coastlines interlock naturally without overlapping.

All 100 islands run through the same pipeline — scale, layout, compaction, and justification — so the result is fully reproducible from the source code.
